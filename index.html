How we work

How ventures get paid for, what has to be true before one moves forward, and when we stop.

Paying for it

ZanCo is funded by its founder. We have not raised money and will not until a venture has customers who would notice if it disappeared. That keeps us from building for a pitch deck.

As ventures start to earn, their profit pays for the next one. The aim is a company that funds itself rather than one that depends on the next round.

Idea to operating business

  • Idea. Can we say who it is for, what it does and why they would use it, in one sentence?
  • Evidence. Have real people asked for it, joined a waitlist, or offered to pay?
  • First version. Does it do one thing well enough that people come back without being reminded?
  • Operating. Does it run without the founder's attention every day, and does the money add up?

Keep going or stop

If a venture cannot find ten people who use it after a full build cycle, we pause it. A change of direction is allowed, but it has to be written down with a reason. A redesign is not a reason.

Stopping is not failure. It frees the time and money for something with better evidence behind it. In a portfolio, a venture that ends is one line in the ledger.

Measured in years

A single start-up has three exits: get bought, float, or close. A holding company does not need any of them. Each venture either earns or teaches, and both feed the next one.

We plan in years, not quarters. The goal is a handful of businesses that are still running, and still ours, a decade from now.
